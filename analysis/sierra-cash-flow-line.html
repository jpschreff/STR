01

What the deduction actually is

Two separate provisions have to stack for this to work, and both are narrower than the internet version.

Bonus depreciation (OBBBA / §168(k)) The building shell itself depreciates over 27.5–39 years no matter what — bonus depreciation never touches it. What bonus depreciation hits is the slice of the purchase price a cost segregation study reclassifies into 5-, 7-, and 15-year property: furniture, appliances, decking, hot tub, landscaping, fencing, driveway, portions of electrical tied to those assets. OBBBA made 100% bonus permanent for property placed in service after 1/19/2025 — that reclassified slice is fully deductible the year the property goes into service, not spread over its class life.
The STR loophole (§469 / Reg. 1.469-1T(e)(3)(ii)) A rental where the average guest stay is 7 days or less isn't a "rental activity" under the passive-loss rules by default — it's tested for material participation like any other business. If either spouse clears a material-participation test (the workable one here: your wife spends more than 100 hours on it and more than anyone else, including any cleaner or manager), the loss is non-passive. That means it offsets W-2, K-1, and other ordinary income directly, with no $25K passive-loss cap and no need for Real Estate Professional status.

The catch nobody mentions: California doesn't conform CA has not adopted federal §168(k) bonus depreciation. You get the full federal deduction on your 1040 — but on your CA 540 that same $300K gets added back and depreciated on the normal schedule instead, over the asset's real class life. The $111K estimate above is a federal-only number. Model the CA side as a multi-year benefit, not a year-one one. This is also exactly why the K-1/STR combination matters: your CA tax bill barely moves, but your federal bill — where the 37% bracket lives — moves a lot.
Excess business loss limit (§461(l), now permanent under OBBBA) 2026 MFJ threshold is $512,000 of net business loss deductible against other income in one year; the rest carries forward as an NOL. At your income level this almost certainly doesn't bind — a $300–375K STR loss nets against $1.6M of positive K-1 business income, so you never show an aggregate business loss in the first place. Confirm with your CPA that the K-1 income is characterized as trade-or-business income (not portfolio/investment income from a fund that isn't itself a trade or business) — that's the one fact that would change this.
What material participation looks like in practice Have your wife be the account holder / co-host of record, personally handle guest messaging, pricing, and booking decisions, and keep a contemporaneous log — dates, hours, task. A full-service property manager who runs pricing and guest comms can put their hours ahead of hers on the "more than anyone else" test and quietly unwind the whole structure. Local cleaner/turnover crew only, self- or co-hosted booking — that's the setup this plan assumes. Year one is the easiest year to clear the hours bar, since furnishing, setup, systems, and listing creation all count.
02

Screening the drivable ring

"Drivable from the Bay Area" and "STR-legal in 2026" turn out to be the real filter — most of the classic weekend-house markets within 4 hours are now capped, waitlisted, or effectively closed to a new buyer this year. Screened against a same-day-drive radius (~4 hrs from San Carlos):

Town of Truckee
Screened out

Town-wide cap of 1,255 permits already reached; new-owner waitlist plus a mandatory 365-day wait after any sale before you can even apply. Dead end for a 2026 placed-in-service date.

Douglas Co., NV (Zephyr Cove / Kingsbury)
Screened out

Township-wide cap of 600, ~44 permits left as of last count, and several sub-neighborhoods (Kingsbury Heights, Zephyr Heights) already full. Permits are non-transferable and expire at sale — you'd be buying a lottery ticket, not a business.

Russian River (Guerneville / Monte Rio)
Screened out

Sonoma County's density-based cap and R1 zoning exclusions have effectively blocked new vacation-rental permits across most of the lower Russian River. Closest market to the Bay Area, but functionally closed to a new entrant.

Pinecrest / Twain Harte corridor
Screened out

Pinecrest itself sits on Forest Service land with tight rental restrictions; the broader Hwy-108 corridor is real but thinner on demand data and further from a ski/lake anchor that drives ADR.

North Lake Tahoe — unincorporated Placer Co. (Kings Beach, Tahoe Vista, Carnelian Bay, Homewood)
Shortlisted

County-wide cap of 3,900 with roughly 300 still open as of mid-2026 — the largest headroom of any capped market in range. Dual ski (Palisades, Northstar) and summer-lake season. ~3h15–3h30 via I-80.

Olympic Valley & Alpine Meadows — unincorporated Placer Co.
Shortlisted, best ADR

Same 3,900-permit Placer County cap and ~300 open as North Lake Tahoe — not a separate pool. Pure ski-resort demand (Palisades Tahoe/Alterra, Ikon Pass, base-to-base gondola linking the two) pushes ADR to ~$564/night, above every other market on this list, while median home prices ($800K–$980K per recent comps) run below South Lake Tahoe and Incline — your $1.6M likely buys more house here than anywhere else on the shortlist. Trade-off: revenue concentrates hard into ski season, so a weak snow year hits harder than a lake town with an independent summer season. Olympic Valley also carries a legacy permit-priority queue from pre-ordinance operators — confirm with Placer County how a new buyer's application is actually sequenced today, not just the county-wide count. Check the specific HOA: Alpine Meadows Estates Association, for one, has its own STR policy layered on top of the county permit. ~3h15–3h30 via I-80, effectively the same drive as Kings Beach.

South Lake Tahoe, CA (El Dorado Co.)
Shortlisted

Residential-zone cap of 900, roughly 300 remaining as of mid-2026 under the new ordinance (verify current count with the city before writing an offer — it moves weekly). Deepest inventory and best comp data of any market in range, but also the most listing-saturated: occupancy was down ~9% YoY even as ADR rose. ~3h45–4h via I-80/US-50.

Incline Village / Crystal Bay, NV (Washoe Co.)
Shortlisted, higher risk/reward

No published hard-number cap — regulated instead by occupancy tier plus parking/septic/defensible-space constraints, so eligibility is parcel-specific and must be confirmed with the county before you're in contract. Thinner supply than either South or North Shore, higher average home values, no NV state income tax (irrelevant to your CA liability, but one less return). ~3h30 via I-80/Hwy-267.

Further afield — north & south of Tahoe/Truckee
These push past the ~4-hour same-day-drive assumption above — call it a long weekend rather than a day trip — but several carry noticeably lighter permitting than anything on the core shortlist, and Yosemite-gateway markets run on a summer/fall demand curve rather than a winter one, which is worth having as a hedge against a bad snow year.

Tuolumne Co. (Pinecrest / Dodge Ridge, Groveland)
Shortlisted, most open market screened

No permit cap and no residency requirement anywhere in the unincorporated county — the lightest-touch regime found in this entire screen, Tahoe or coastal. Only requirements are a fire/life-safety inspection and a TOT certificate (effective Jan 2024). Groveland rides Yosemite gateway demand rather than ski demand; Pinecrest/Dodge Ridge is the ski-anchored option but Pinecrest itself sits partly on Forest Service land with its own rental restrictions — confirm parcel status before assuming county rules are the only layer. ~3h15 (Groveland) to ~3h45–4h (Pinecrest) via I-580/Hwy-120 or Hwy-108.

Alpine Co. (Kirkwood / Bear Valley)
Shortlisted, no cap found

STRs explicitly allowed under a county ordinance updated April 2024; no numeric permit cap surfaced, unlike every Mono County ski market. Kirkwood and Bear Valley are each carved out of the standard neighbor-notification requirement, suggesting the county treats these resort-specific plan areas as a separate, friendlier track. Separate county from Placer/El Dorado, so it isn't sharing North Lake Tahoe's permit pool. Confirm current status directly with Alpine County — it's a small planning department and rules can move fast. ~3h30–4h via Hwy-88 (Kirkwood) or Hwy-4 (Bear Valley).

Mariposa Co. (Yosemite Gateway, incl. Wawona)
Watchlist, verify directly

Hosted and unhosted STRs both allowed under a "comprehensive" county framework, but no cap number surfaced in research and standards vary by location and property type. Wawona specifically requires concurrent county + National Park Service permitting — a second approval layer not present anywhere else on this list. Call county planning before treating this as open. ~3h30–4h via Hwy-140/Hwy-41.

June Lake, Mono Co.
Shortlisted, hard-capped

New ordinance (effective 2/23/2026) sets a numeric cap of 95 STR permits countywide, plus a 2-year wait before a newly built or newly sold unit can apply. Non-owner-occupied STRs are otherwise banned across Mono County by emergency moratorium except two June Lake neighborhoods — so this is one of the only paths into the county for an absentee owner. ~5–6h via Hwy-395, well past a same-day-drive weekend house.

Mammoth Lakes, Mono Co.
Shortlisted, program-managed

2022's moratorium was lifted April 2024 and replaced with a "Certified Properties" program — existing permit holders continue, new permits issue based on zoning and compliance rather than a hard countable number. Functionally similar diligence to South Lake Tahoe's "check weekly" screen. Strongest ADR and demand depth of anything on this extended list, given Mammoth Mountain's draw, but also the longest drive: ~5–6h via Hwy-395.

Unresolved: Plumas Co. (Graeagle, Lake Almanor) & Sierra Co. (Sierra City, Downieville) No permit-cap information surfaced for either county — not screened out, just unverified. Both sit ~4–4.5h from the Bay Area, similar drive-time tier to Mammoth. Worth a direct call to each county's planning department before ruling in or out.

What this extension adds to the picture Tuolumne and Mariposa land closest to base-case breakeven of any market screened in this document — not because revenue is higher, but because purchase price is meaningfully lower, the same dynamic already flagged for Olympic Valley vs. South Lake Tahoe. June Lake and Mammoth instead repeat the core shortlist's pattern: base case negative, upside case solidly positive, with the bonus-depreciation shield doing the same bridging work. None of these five change the conclusion in Section 03 above — they widen the property search, they don't replace the underlying math.
The finding that should drive the decision Stretching to the $2.0M/20%-down tier does not pay for itself — the extra $400K of debt service outruns the extra revenue a bigger house commands, even at the upside case. The $1.6M/25%-down tier gets meaningfully closer to breakeven at every market. Incline Village's lower property tax gets closest on this model, with Olympic Valley/Alpine Meadows a close second on cash flow and arguably the better pick on total value — lower comparable home prices there mean the same $1.6M likely buys more house (and more achievable revenue) than the model's flat scenario captures. Put the $400K to work as a bigger down payment on a cheaper, better-positioned house rather than the down payment on a more expensive one.
How the tax shield actually functions here None of the scenarios above are reliably operating-cash-flow-positive in year one. The ~$111–140K federal tax savings from bonus depreciation isn't a rounding error on top of that — it's the thing that converts a $10–35K/year operating shortfall into a strongly cash-positive year one, deliverable in practice as a reduced Q3/Q4 2026 estimated payment once the cost seg study and CPA confirm the number. From year two on, the deduction is much smaller (ordinary straight-line only), so the property needs to stand closer to its own two feet — which is exactly why the sub-market and property selection below still matter more than the depreciation math, which is roughly proportional to price everywhere in Tahoe.

Timeline to a 2026 placed-in-service date

Today is August 19, 2026. "Placed in service" means available and listed for rent, not necessarily already booked — but permit turnaround and furnishing still take real weeks, and every one of the shortlisted markets requires a fresh permit application after closing.

Now – Early Sept

Engage a Tahoe-specialist buyer's agent and a cost segregation firm in parallel; confirm live permit counts in all three shortlisted sub-markets; get pre-approved for a second-home loan sized to the $1.6M tier.

Sept

Offer and go under contract. Where the local custom supports it, make the offer contingent on confirming the specific parcel's STR-permit eligibility before removing contingencies.

Oct

Close escrow. File the STR permit application the same week as closing — this is usually the longest pole in the tent.

Oct – Nov

Furnish and set up (these costs land in the cost-seg-eligible bucket); order the cost segregation study once furnishing is substantially complete; set up the listing with your wife as host/co-host of record.

By Dec 31

Property listed and available to book — placed in service for 2026. Begin the contemporaneous hours log immediately, not retroactively.

Q1 2027

CPA finalizes the cost segregation allocation and federal bonus depreciation figure on the 2026 return; true up estimated payments.

06

Before you act on any of this

  • This is a modeling framework, not a listing search. I don't have access to live MLS inventory — once you have candidate addresses, send them over and I can pencil the actual numbers against this model.
  • Confirm the K-1 income's character with your CPA before relying on the §461(l) analysis — the aggregation only works cleanly if it's trade-or-business income.
  • Order the cost segregation study from a firm that does engineering-based studies, not a percentage-estimate mill — the IRS has specifically flagged low-quality STR cost seg studies for audit scrutiny.
  • Recapture is real. Bonus depreciation is accelerated, not free — selling before death or a 1031 exchange triggers unrecaptured §1250 gain on everything you deducted. Plan the hold period accordingly.
  • Re-verify every permit count and insurance assumption at time of offer — every number in this document is a snapshot as of mid-2026 and several of these programs are moving targets.
